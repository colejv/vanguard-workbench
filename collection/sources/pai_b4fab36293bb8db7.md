Modern Command and Control is measured by how fast you can sense, decide, and act — together. Because the side that acts first wins. At Lightning Surge 2, alongside the 25th ID, CPE C2IN, and our industry partners Lockheed Martin, Accelint, and Rune Technologies, we put the Army’s NGC2 fighting prototype to the test. Raft Data Platform and Raft AI Mission System served as the unified data layer and AI backbone inside that prototype. During the exercise, electronic warfare targeting data, live drone video, positional feeds, and battle damage assessments were ingested and fused in real time through Raft Data Platform; eliminating manual cross-system reconciliation and delivering a unified, mission-ready operational picture to soldiers at the tactical edge. Through Raft AI Mission System, soldiers issued voice commands to automate fires workflows, accelerating airspace deconfliction, target validation, and clearance processes that traditionally require layered coordination. The result was compressed time-to-shoot under live-fire stress. As HIMARS and M777 artillery executed missions, ammunition expenditure updated live and linked directly to sustainment systems, giving commanders real-time visibility into combat power and predictive resupply requirements during active engagements. Lightning Surge 2 demonstrated a complete, integrated sensor-to-shooter thread and validated that a hardware- and data-agnostic architecture, operationalized with AI-powered workflows, materially reduces latency, integrates best-of-breed technologies, and performs under real operational complexity at the tactical edge.       For decades, the military has faced the same problem: systems, hardware, COPs, and teams that don’t talk to each other. Operators manually move data between multiple systems just to piece together the fight. That’s the status quo today. Now put that same process in a contested environment where networks are unreliable, systems lose connection, and the information operators rely on can disappear without warning, when the mission is at stake and lives are on the line. Enter the US Army’s Next Generation Command and Control (NGC2) initiative. During Lightning Surge 1 and 2, Raft Data Platform and Raft AI Mission System powered the 25th Infantry Division and U.S. Army Hawai'i’s NGC2 fighting prototype. Raft Data Platform ([R]DP) is the data integration layer, ingesting and fusing multi-domain battlefield data, including electronic warfare targeting, live drone video, positional feeds, and battle damage assessments, into a unified, mission-ready operating picture. Raft AI Mission System ([R]AIMS) is the AI backbone, transforming that data into contextualized information through AI-powered workflows and voice-enabled agentic perception that accelerated decision loops at the tactical edge. Together, [R]DP and [R]AIMS enabled real-time sensor-to-shooter execution. Soldiers cleared airspace, cued fires, and executed HIMARS and M777 artillery engagements using live battlefield data. [R]DP and [R]AIMS also enabled predictive sustainment, tracking ammunition consumption in real time and forecasting resupply needs tied directly to combat operations. As we move toward Lightning Surge 3, alongside our partners Lockheed Martin, Rune Technologies, and Accelint, federated and agnostic data integration layers like [R]DP and perception-driven AI mission systems like [R]AIMS will continue enabling a future where battlefield systems are integrated, data moves at the speed of the fight, and commanders can see, decide, and act faster than the adversary. #LFG 🚀 Read the full article: https://lnkd.in/ebiXA_xA To view or add a comment, sign in

      My 2 cents on why the Army's NGC2 approach at 25th ID has been so powerful: 1) User focus. We're deploying and working directly alongside users. We're not building in a lab or creating BS PPT slides. This is user-led. 2) Data agnostic. Take the data as it is, normalize and transform it as required via Raft Data Platform in milliseconds, then SEND IT. This creates a low barrier of entry for legacy and modern software applications. 3) Product driven. We're not building from scratch. The Army is starting with existing, proven, ATO'd products (RDP & RAIMS). This allowed us to deploy in days, not weeks, months, or years. 4) Edge first. We build backwards from the edge. What does the edge user need? Solve that, then move to the next. 5) Hardware agnostic. We deploy on whatever piece of metal or cloud the Army hands us. 5) Accountability. Either you deliver or you're out. No multi-year T&M science project measured by lagging indicators like CPARs. Either your stuff works or we move on and find someone else who can deliver. That's how business works. As a taxpayer and patriot, I'm glad to see the government adopting this approach.
